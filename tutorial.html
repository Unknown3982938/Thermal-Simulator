01 — GETTING STARTED

Opening the Laboratory

The Thermodynamics Laboratory runs directly inside a compatible web browser. No conventional software installation is required.

  1. Open the project's published website.
  2. Wait for the simulation interface to finish loading.
  3. Select the desired experimental mode.
  4. Adjust the available controls to observe changes.
ⓘ

An active internet connection may be required to load external libraries used by the simulation.

02 — INTERFACE OVERVIEW

Understanding the Interface

The simulation interface is organized into separate visual and control areas.

3D Simulation Viewport

Displays the simulated thermodynamic system, including the container, particles, piston, and other visual elements.

Control Panel

Contains interactive controls for changing simulation parameters and selecting experimental configurations.

Information Display

Presents numerical values and other information associated with the current simulation.

Simulation Controls

Use the available buttons and sliders to modify parameters and observe the resulting behavior. Available controls may differ between modes.

Gas Laws: Basic Relationships

Gas behavior can be described through relationships between pressure, volume, and absolute temperature.

PV = nRT
  • P — Pressure
  • V — Volume
  • n — Amount of substance
  • R — Ideal gas constant
  • T — Absolute temperature

This is the ideal gas equation. It describes the relationship among these variables under the assumptions of the ideal gas model.

Phase Change: Understanding States of Matter

Matter can exist in different physical states. Changes in energy and temperature can influence particle movement and arrangement.

  • Solid: Particles remain organized around relatively fixed positions.
  • Liquid: Particles remain close together but can move around one another.
  • Gas: Particles move more freely and occupy the available space.
ⓘ

During a phase transition of a pure substance at constant pressure, energy transfer can change its physical state without changing its temperature.

First Law: Conservation of Energy

The first law of thermodynamics describes how energy is conserved in a thermodynamic system.

ΔU = Q − W
  • ΔU — Change in internal energy
  • Q — Heat added to the system
  • W — Work done by the system

This equation uses the convention that heat entering the system is positive and work done by the system is positive.

06 — EXPERIMENT TIPS

Getting Better Results

  1. Start with the default simulation settings.
  2. Identify which physical quantity you want to investigate.
  3. Change one parameter at a time when studying its effect.
  4. Observe the visual changes and numerical readings.
  5. Compare the results with the relevant thermodynamic principle.
  6. Repeat the experiment using different parameter values.
✦

Try to predict what will happen before changing a parameter. Comparing your prediction with the simulation's response makes the experiment more useful.

07 — TROUBLESHOOTING

Common Issues

Simulation does not load

Check your internet connection and reload the page. External libraries may not have loaded successfully.

3D graphics are missing

Make sure your browser supports WebGL and that hardware acceleration is available.

Controls do not respond

Reload the simulation and check whether the interface has finished initializing.

Performance is slow

Close unnecessary browser tabs and applications. Performance may vary depending on your device's graphics capabilities.

Final Note

The Thermodynamics Laboratory is intended to supplement the study of thermodynamics through interactive visualization. Always refer to established scientific materials when performing detailed calculations or interpreting experimental results.
